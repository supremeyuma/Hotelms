# Front desk arrival cutoff — how the hotel now handles bookings

Read this if you work the front desk at Harbour Hotel. It explains one rule we
have introduced and how to handle a guest it affects.

## What changed

The desk and the website share the same rooms. Before, a guest could book a
room online for a date the desk had already sold, and two people would end up
holding the same room. To stop that, **one side owns every arrival date**:

| Arrival | Handled by |
|---|---|
| Now, tonight, tomorrow | The front desk |
| Further out (past the cutoff) | The online booking channel |

The boundary is a number of hours, called the **arrival cutoff**. Its default is
**48 hours**. Because a check-in date has no time of day, the desk effectively
covers arrivals up to about 72 hours out — in other words, **now and tomorrow,
and usually the day after as well**. Arrivals beyond that are taken on the
online channel, not at the desk.

This means the desk will now **refuse** (with a clear message) to sell a room for
an arrival that is further out than the cutoff. That is deliberate. The front
desk holds the freshest room state in the building, and giving one side (and
only one side) the right to sell each arrival prevents two people selling the
same room.

## What to do when a guest wants a room further out

Two options, whichever the guest prefers:

1. **Walk them through booking online** — same room types, same rates. The
   website is the right place for a far-out arrival.
2. **Take a deposit and finalise it on the cloud** — hold the guest's details,
   take a deposit at the desk, and create the booking through the cloud/online
   facilities. Do **not** create it as a desk booking.

## Walk-ins are not affected

A guest who walks in today still gets the best path. The desk books them
immediately, because the desk's copy of the room state is the freshest there is.
The cutoff only redirects **future** arrivals.

## Checking the cutoff

The cutoff is a setting ("Front desk arrival window"), visible on the booking
page and the reservations list. Any manager can change it in
**Settings → Front Desk Arrival Window → Arrival cutoff (hours)**, no code
change needed. For example:

- A property with guests driving from the airport may use **24** (tonight and
  tomorrow).
- One with online demand from further away may keep **48** (reaching the day
  after).

Raise it or lower it as the property needs.

## If a guest is upset about it

The refusal is the system protecting the hotel from overbooking. Tell the guest
the room is available online for their date, and offer to walk them through it
there and then, or take a deposit and finalise it on the cloud. If a guest is
still unhappy, note their name and details and tell the manager — the cutoff
can be adjusted, and the manager decides when a specific guest should be
booked around it.
